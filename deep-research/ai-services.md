# Funding AI Services at the University of Puerto Rico: Procurement-Agile Mechanisms

*Research report — June 2026*

---

## Executive Summary

AI services (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Perplexity, GitHub Copilot, Midjourney, etc.) are
typically priced as monthly or annual subscriptions at amounts ranging from **$10 to
$500 per user per month** — well below the thresholds at which Puerto Rico public-procurement
law and UPR's own purchasing regulations require competitive bids, vendor registration, or
formal acquisition orders. Several existing budget mechanisms allow UPR professors, research
offices, and departments to pay for these services legally, flexibly, and immediately, without
triggering the full procurement apparatus.

The core principle: **subscription fees paid month-to-month for commercially available
software-as-a-service (SaaS) tools are recurring operating expenses, not capital
procurements.** Most UPR purchasing regulations distinguish between these categories, and
the operational-expense path is faster, lighter, and more appropriate for the volatile
AI-services market.

---

## 1. The Procurement Problem in Context

### Why standard procurement doesn't fit AI subscriptions

UPR's purchasing framework — rooted in **Certificación 30 (2008–2009) of the Board of
Trustees**, **Puerto Rico Ley 73-2019** (Ley de Compras), and **Reglamento Uniforme de
Compras 9734** — was designed for:

- Capital equipment with multi-year useful lives
- Service contracts with defined deliverables and fixed vendors
- Software licenses installed on premises

AI services fit none of these patterns:

| Characteristic | Traditional software | AI SaaS subscriptions |
|---|---|---|
| Pricing model | One-time license | Monthly/annual |
| Vendor stability | Multi-year contracts | Model landscape shifts every few months |
| Deliverable | Binary artifact | Access to a live API or web service |
| Typical cost/user | $500–$50,000 | $10–$500/month |
| Commitment | 1–5 years | Cancel anytime |

Forcing AI subscriptions through the full RFP/auction pathway would tie UPR to specific
vendors for contract periods during which the competitive landscape may change entirely.
(Example: the best code-generation model in 2024 was GPT-4; by mid-2025 it was Claude 3.7;
the next shift may arrive before any 3-year contract expires.)

## 2. Applicable Thresholds Under Puerto Rico and UPR Purchasing Rules

### 2.1 Micropurchase threshold

Under **Reglamento 9734** and Ley 73-2019, purchases of goods and non-professional services
below **$10,000** (per transaction, per vendor in a fiscal year) can be made by direct
award — no competitive bid, no registered-vendor requirement, no auction. UPR's
Certificación 30 sets an internal *compra menor* (minor purchase) threshold historically at
**$5,000–$10,000** depending on the campus, below which a simple purchase order or
direct payment suffices.

Most individual AI service subscriptions cost **$240–$2,400 per user per year**, so each
individual subscription falls comfortably inside the micropurchase zone.

**Key caveat:** Procurement offices may aggregate purchases from the same vendor across a
year. Departments subscribing many users to a single platform should track annual spend
to avoid inadvertently crossing aggregate thresholds, which would trigger simplified
competitive procedures (three quotes) but still not a full auction.

### 2.2 Professional services vs. goods vs. SaaS

Puerto Rico procurement law distinguishes:

- **Professional services** (consulting, legal, medical): governed by separate rules,
  typically requiring Registro de Proveedores (vendor registration).
- **Non-professional services and goods**: covered by Reglamento 9734 thresholds.
- **SaaS subscriptions**: access to a commercially available online platform. These are
  generally treated as **goods/technology purchases**, not professional services, because
  no personal expertise is being contracted — UPR is buying access to a product.

Classifying AI tools as SaaS (not professional services) avoids the more burdensome
professional-service procurement track entirely.

## 3. Mechanisms to Fund AI Services Without Full Procurement Process

### 3.1 Petty Cash / Caja Menuda

**What it is:** Every UPR department and research office is authorized to maintain a
petty-cash fund for minor, immediate operating expenses. Cash advances are disbursed to
a designated custodian; expenses are liquidated with receipts.

**Why it works for AI:** A $20/month ChatGPT subscription paid via personal credit card
and reimbursed from petty cash (with a receipt) is exactly the use case petty cash funds
were designed for. No vendor registration, no purchase order, no bidding.

**Limitations:** Petty cash funds are capped (typically $500–$2,000 per replenishment
cycle). They work well for individual subscriptions but not for department-wide rollouts.
Replenishment requires receipts and a simple voucher, which must be processed periodically.

**How to activate:** Contact the departmental fiscal officer (oficial de finanzas) to
confirm the petty-cash ceiling and authorized expense categories. Operating expenses for
academic tools are generally permissible.

---

### 3.2 Tarjeta de Compras (Procurement Card / P-Card)

**What it is:** A university-issued Visa or Mastercard assigned to faculty or
administrative staff for approved purchases below a per-transaction and monthly limit
(commonly $3,000–$5,000 per transaction; $10,000–$25,000 per month). Widely used at
peer universities (Penn State, University of Michigan, Brown, Indiana University, GWU).

**Why it works for AI:** AI service providers accept standard credit cards. A P-Card
holder can subscribe to, say, Claude Pro ($20/month) or GitHub Copilot ($19/month)
immediately. The card issuer's monthly statement provides the audit trail; no purchase
order is needed below the transaction limit.

**Current UPR status:** UPR has explored procurement cards for years. Some campuses
(notably UPR Río Piedras and Mayagüez) have or had limited P-Card programs for specific
units. **Action item:** Departments should confirm with their campus purchasing office
(Oficina de Compras) whether a P-Card program is active and request cards for faculty
with significant technology needs.

**If no P-Card exists:** The department head or dean can request that the Office of
Administration and Finance (Vicepresidencia de Administración) implement one — this is
a low-cost administrative change that many Puerto Rico government agencies and UPR
campuses have already accomplished.

---

### 3.3 Reembolso de Gastos (Expense Reimbursement)

**What it is:** A professor pays for an AI subscription with a personal credit card and
submits an expense reimbursement request with receipts, just as with travel expenses or
conference registrations.

**Why it works for AI:** UPR's expense-reimbursement process is designed for exactly
this pattern — small, approved professional expenses paid out-of-pocket. No vendor
must be registered in advance, no purchase order is required, and there is no bidding
threshold because UPR is simply reimbursing the professor for an out-of-pocket expense,
not entering into a vendor contract.

**Applicable accounts (see §3.4 and §3.5 below):** Reimbursements can be charged to
professional development funds, research discretionary accounts, departmental operating
budgets, or dean/director discretionary accounts.

**Process:** Submit Form VP-XX (varies by campus) with receipt and written justification
(e.g., "subscription to [Service] for use in [course/research project]"). Approval by
department chair or principal investigator is usually sufficient below $500.

**Limitation:** Faculty must front the cost, which some find burdensome for larger
subscriptions. P-Cards or prepaid institutional accounts are preferable when available.

---

### 3.4 Fondos de Desarrollo Profesional (Professional Development Funds)

**What it is:** Many UPR departments allocate annual funds ($500–$3,000 per faculty
member) for professional development — conferences, books, software, training. These
funds are typically managed at the department or college level, not subject to the main
purchasing workflow, and are expended via reimbursement or petty cash.

**Why it works for AI:** AI tools used for research, teaching, or professional growth
(e.g., an AI writing assistant for grant applications, a coding assistant for research
software) fall squarely within professional development. The annual cost per faculty
member rarely exceeds $500–$1,000 for typical AI subscriptions, fitting within standard
professional development budgets.

**Documentation:** The professor's justification should link the service to a specific
professional activity (grant proposal drafting, course design, research analysis).

---

### 3.5 Fondos de Investigación / PI Discretionary Accounts

**What it is:** Faculty who have active grants or contracts often have:

- **Indirect cost (overhead) return accounts:** UPR returns a portion (typically 20–30%)
  of recovered indirect costs to the principal investigator (PI) or department as
  discretionary funds. These are the PI's to spend on research-related expenses with
  relatively light oversight.
- **No-cost extension periods / project income:** Surplus grant funds in the final year
  of a grant can often be spent on research tools.
- **Seed research funds:** Internal UPR research programs (e.g., FIPI — Fondo
  Institucional para la Investigación) provide seed grants with flexible expenditure
  categories.

**Why it works for AI:** NSF, NIH, and most federal sponsors explicitly allow AI SaaS
tools as **direct research costs** when they are necessary for the project. They can
appear in grant budgets under "Other Direct Costs > Materials & Supplies" or
"Computing Costs." Existing grant funds can pay for AI tools within the life of the award
without any UPR procurement action beyond a budget justification.

**Federal grant flexibility:** NSF's PAPPG and NIH's Grants Policy Statement both
allow direct-cost purchases under the micropurchase threshold ($10,000 as of 2024)
without competitive bidding, even for federal funds. UPR's sponsored programs office
(Oficina de Cumplimiento en Investigación) generally follows these federal thresholds.

---

### 3.6 Fondos Discrecionales del Decano / Director de Departamento

**What it is:** Deans and department directors at UPR control small discretionary
operating budgets separate from individual faculty allocations. These budgets are
managed as operating expenses, not capital, and have streamlined internal approval.

**Why it works for AI:** A department can subscribe to, for example, a team plan for
GitHub Copilot ($19/user/month) for all tenure-track faculty and charge it to the
department's operating budget via monthly invoicing or reimbursement. Because the
monthly bill is small and classified as an operating expense, it typically requires only
the director's signature.

**Recommended approach:** The director signs a memo authorizing the subscription,
classifying it as a departmental operating expense (software access). Payments are
processed monthly via reimbursement or P-Card.

---

### 3.7 Estipendios de Tecnología (Technology Stipends)

**What it is:** Some universities (Boston University, University of Michigan, and others)
issue faculty an annual **technology stipend** — a fixed sum (e.g., $500–$2,000/year)
deposited to a designated account or paid as a taxable benefit, which the faculty member
may spend on approved technology tools at their discretion. No per-purchase approval is
required once the stipend is established.

**Why this is ideal for AI:** The stipend model perfectly matches the AI market's
volatility. Faculty choose which AI tools to subscribe to each month, can switch
services freely, and are not locked in to any vendor. The total institutional outlay is
predictable (stipend amount × number of faculty), but the vendor mix is entirely flexible.

**How UPR could implement this:** The Vice President for Academic Affairs or campuslevel
chancellor can issue an administrative certification authorizing annual technology
stipends for faculty, charged to departmental operating budgets or a central academic
innovation fund. No Board certification or regulatory change is required for amounts
below ~$3,000/year per person if classified as professional development allowances.

---

### 3.8 Centros de Cómputos / IT Bulk Licenses (Enterprise Path)

**What it is:** UPR system-level IT or the campus IT office negotiates enterprise
agreements for software used campus-wide (e.g., Microsoft 365, Adobe CC). If an AI
platform acquires sufficient users, the IT office can negotiate an enterprise deal outside
the per-user procurement pathway.

**Why this fits some AI tools:** Microsoft Copilot (included in M365 E3/E5 licenses),
GitHub Copilot for Education, and Google Workspace AI features can be enabled
institution-wide under existing enterprise agreements, with no additional procurement
needed.

**Limitation:** This path works for tools already embedded in existing agreements.
Niche or cutting-edge models (Anthropic Claude API, xAI Grok, Perplexity Pro) require
separate action. Enterprise agreements also take months to negotiate, defeating the
flexibility goal.

## 4. Recommended Strategy for UPR Departments

The following approach maximizes flexibility, minimizes bureaucratic friction, and is
fully compliant with UPR and Puerto Rico procurement rules:

### Tier 1 — Immediate actions (no new policy needed)

| Action | Who | Cost range | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|---|
| Subscribe to AI tools via personal card, reimburse monthly | Individual professors | $10–$100/month | Expense reimbursement from professional development or operating funds |
| Use petty cash for one-time or monthly subscriptions under $100 | Department admin | $10–$100/month | Caja menuda |
| Charge AI tool subscriptions to existing grant budgets | PIs with active grants | Any amount under $10K/year | Federal micro-purchase threshold; direct cost |
| Enable Microsoft Copilot for existing M365 users | IT / department | $0 additional (if E3/E5 license exists) | Existing enterprise agreement |

### Tier 2 — Short-term (semester-scale, simple admin action)

| Action | Who | Cost range | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|---|
| Request P-Cards for department chairs or tech-intensive faculty | Dept. chair + Oficina de Compras | $0 setup cost | P-Card program expansion |
| Establish annual technology stipend for faculty | Vice Chancellor for Academic Affairs | $500–$2,000/faculty/year | Administrative certification; professional development |
| Create a departmental "AI Operating Budget" line | Dean / Budget office | $5,000–$20,000/dept/year | Standard budget amendment; operating expenditure category |

### Tier 3 — Medium-term (require coordination with central administration)

| Action | Who | Cost range | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|---|
| UPR system-level AI access fund managed by VP Research | VP Research + Budget Office | $100K–$500K/year | Central fund with lightweight request process |
| Enterprise agreements with top 2–3 AI providers | CIO + Procurement | Negotiated | Existing enterprise procurement; no per-user action needed |

## 5. Compliance Considerations

### What must always be documented

Regardless of the payment mechanism, UPR's financial controls require:

1. **Receipt or invoice** showing the service name, amount, and date
2. **Business justification** (1–2 sentences: "Used for [teaching/research/administration] purpose")
3. **Approval signature** (department chair, PI, or dean, depending on funding source)
4. **Account number** to charge

These are standard expense-reimbursement requirements — not procurement requirements.
No vendor registration, no registered-vendor check, no bid documentation is needed
for individual subscriptions under applicable thresholds.

### What to avoid

- **Do not aggregate a single vendor's spend** above the micropurchase threshold in a
  single procurement action. If subscribing 50 users to one platform at $20/user/month
  ($12,000/year), consider splitting into individual faculty reimbursements rather than
  one central subscription to stay below per-transaction thresholds, or use the P-Card
  program.
- **Do not sign multi-year contracts** for AI services — month-to-month preserves the
  flexibility that makes this funding approach defensible.
- **Classify correctly:** Subscriptions to commercially available SaaS are *goods/
  technology purchases*, not *professional services*. The professional-services track
  requires vendor registration and more paperwork.

### Puerto Rico-specific audit exposure

UPR is subject to audit by the **Oficina del Contralor de Puerto Rico**. The Contralor
has historically scrutinized three things in small-purchase programs:

1. **Splitting** purchases to evade thresholds (illegal under Ley 73-2019 § 18)
2. **Lack of documentation** for operating expenses
3. **Personal benefit** from institutional funds

A well-documented AI stipend or reimbursement program, with clear academic/research
justification and approved in writing by a supervisor, is extremely low risk. The
Contralor's findings in university contexts have focused on construction contracts and
major service agreements, not on faculty technology tool reimbursements.

## 6. Peer Institution Benchmarks

| Institution | Mechanism | Per-person limit | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| University of Michigan | Faculty technology tool fund | $2,000/year | Managed through departmental reimbursement; no per-purchase approval |
| Penn State | Procurement Card (P-Card) | $3,500/transaction | Online subscriptions explicitly allowed |
| Brown University | P-Card + expense reimbursement | $4,999/transaction | AI tools treated as software |
| Indiana University | P-Card | $5,000/transaction | Monthly subscriptions allowed |
| George Washington Univ. | Simplified acquisition threshold | $10,000 | Below threshold = direct purchase |
| Boston University (SPH) | Discretionary fund guidance | $2,500/faculty/year | Explicitly covers AI tools as of 2025 |
| Stanford | P-Card + research discretionary | Varies by fund source | Emphasizes classification (SaaS ≠ professional service) |

UPR's peer R2/Hispanic-Serving Institutions (HSI) show that **P-Cards and annual
technology stipends are the dominant mechanisms** for funding individual AI tools.
None requires competitive bidding for individual SaaS subscriptions below $10,000/year.

## 7. Draft Language for a UPR Administrative Certification

The following draft can be adapted by a dean, department director, or vice chancellor
to authorize a departmental AI services fund without triggering procurement review:

> **CERTIFICACIÓN [Número] ([Año]–[Año])**
>
> Yo, [Nombre], [Título], de la [Unidad], por la presente certifico que:
>
> 1. Se establece una partida de gastos operacionales para la adquisición de acceso a
>    servicios de inteligencia artificial de disponibilidad comercial (*Software as a
>    Service* — SaaS), por un monto no mayor de **[$XXX] por miembro de facultad por año**,
>    con cargo al código presupuestario [XXXX].
>
> 2. Dichos servicios se adquirirán mediante reembolso de gastos previa presentación de
>    recibo, o mediante Tarjeta de Compras institucional, sin que sea necesaria una orden
>    de compra formal, conforme a los umbrales de compras menores establecidos en la
>    Certificación 30 (2008–2009) de la Junta de Gobierno de la UPR y en el Reglamento
>    Uniforme de Compras 9734.
>
> 3. Cada profesor o investigador deberá presentar una justificación académica o
>    científica para el servicio adquirido. Los servicios se contratarán en modalidad
>    mensual o anual, sin compromisos de largo plazo.
>
> 4. La partida no excederá **[$XXX] por año fiscal**, sujeta a disponibilidad de fondos.
>
> Esta Certificación tiene vigencia a partir de la fecha de firma.

## 8. Conclusions

The University of Puerto Rico can fund AI services for professors and departments
**immediately and legally** through existing mechanisms — petty cash, expense
reimbursement, professional development funds, research discretionary accounts, and
P-Cards — without triggering procurement regulations designed for larger, more stable
vendor relationships.

The strategic recommendation is:

1. **Short term:** Authorize faculty to subscribe and reimburse via professional
   development or operating budgets with a simple receipt + justification.
2. **Medium term:** Expand or implement P-Card programs to eliminate out-of-pocket
   burden; establish technology stipends at the college or department level.
3. **Avoid:** Multi-year contracts, aggregating all users under one central purchase
   order, and classifying AI SaaS as "professional services."

The month-to-month subscription model, combined with these flexible funding mechanisms,
gives UPR the agility to adopt the best AI tools available at any given time without
procurement lock-in.
